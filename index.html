About
01 — ABOUT

I'm a civil engineer with a background in film and a growing obsession with how people interact with technology.

I have a Master's in Human-Computer Interaction from KTH, where I've explored that intersection through UX and UI design, research, analysis, prototyping and user testing. I like taking an idea from something vague and hard to explain into something you can actually interact with — and then seeing what happens when someone else tries to use it.

I'm curious about the relationship between people and technology, and about the difference between how something is supposed to work and how it actually works in the real world.

/ UX · AI

Published Projects

As part of a research project at KTH, I explored how AI could support reading comprehension by providing personalised feedback based on how people read.

The project combined AI-generated feedback with cursor tracking to analyse reading behaviour and identify potential difficulties. Through interaction design, prototyping and user evaluation, we investigated how AI could be used as a learning companion rather than simply a tool for providing answers.

The work resulted in a research publication and gave me hands-on experience in designing and evaluating AI-driven interactions.

Background
03 — BACKGROUND

Working in film and television for over a decade has taught me a lot. And surprisingly, not all of it has been about cameras, cables and technology.

Film sets can be pretty chaotic places. Plans change, things break, time is always running out and somehow everything still needs to come together. Over the years, I've learned to keep a cool head when things get stressful, figure things out as I go and find solutions even when there isn't an obvious one. I've also learned that sometimes the best thing you can do is take a moment, look at the bigger picture and not make a small problem into a bigger one.

One of my favourite parts of working in production has always been the people. I've worked with all sorts of personalities, from small crews where everyone does a bit of everything to large international productions with hundreds of people involved. It's taught me to listen, read the room and adjust to different people and situations. Sometimes you need to speak up and take charge, sometimes you need to step back and let someone else do their thing. Knowing the difference is something you learn along the way.

I also believe there's a lot to be said for simply being someone people enjoy working with. Showing up prepared, doing what you say you'll do, owning your mistakes and helping out when someone needs a hand, even if it's not technically your responsibility. And when it's been a long day and nothing seems to be going according to plan, a little patience and a sense of humour can make all the difference.

After all these years, I still enjoy being thrown into something new. New teams, unfamiliar equipment, unexpected challenges and having to learn things on the fly. I like figuring out how things work, asking questions and finding better ways of doing things. I'm not particularly interested in doing something a certain way just because that's how it's always been done.

I think that's probably what I've taken with me most from my years in production. Be curious, be adaptable, take your work seriously but don't take yourself too seriously, and try to leave things a little better than you found them.

And those are things I hope to bring with me wherever I end up next.
